This project was part of my dissertation, which explores ways and methods of reproducing photorealistic results in real-time game engines for their use in archaeological sites and monuments. The goal was to achieve a three-dimensional interactive depiction of the Stoa of Philip V in Delos.

In order to achieve the digital restoration with historical accuracy, it was necessary to research and study the history of the stoa and the architecture of the construction — beginning with the architectural plans and the materials used to build it. The next phase was the creation of the three-dimensional model of the Stoa and its environment. The representation was built in Unreal Engine 4 using PBR textures and visual scripting (blueprints).

The main objective was to convey to viewers the actual Stoa of Philip V, and to demonstrate ways of reproducing photorealistic results in real-time engines for use at archaeological sites and monuments. The Stoa was created according to information given by the archaeologists in charge of its restoration. The surrounding environment was created in approximation to reality, so that viewers get a general idea of what it might have looked like; further research would be needed for more detail.

The completed project produced photographs, video, and an executable program that can be used to correctly visualise the Stoa of Philip V in Delos.
